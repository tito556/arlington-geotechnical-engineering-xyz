Electrical Resistivity Surveys and Vertical Electrical Sounding (VES) in Arlington, TX

Together, we solve the challenges of tomorrow.

LEARN MORE →

ASTM D6431 sets the standard for electrical resistivity imaging, but applying it effectively in Arlington means understanding the local geology. The Woodbine Formation and Quaternary terrace deposits underlying much of the city create a specific geoelectrical signature that we’ve learned to interpret over dozens of projects. Subsurface conditions here shift within a few hundred feet: sandy paleochannels pinch out against weathered Eagle Ford shale, and perched water tables appear where you least expect them. A MASW survey often complements the resistivity data by adding shear-wave velocity profiles—useful when you need both stratigraphy and Vs30 site classification under the IBC. For deeper foundation design, especially where soft shale interfaces could compromise bearing, our pile testing group uses the resistivity cross-sections to plan boring locations precisely, reducing the number of invasive drill holes across the site.

Resistivity in Arlington is most diagnostic when the target contrasts with native shale: sand lenses, water-filled fractures, and karst cavities all jump out of the inverted profile.

Methodology and scope

A recent project on a 14-acre commercial site near Lake Arlington illustrated the method’s strengths. The geotechnical report flagged irregular bedrock depth, but drilling budget limited the client to eight borings. We ran four VES lines at 120-meter spacing using a Wenner array, and the inverted model revealed a buried channel incised into the shale—undetected by the original grid. The resistivity contrast between saturated sand (45 ohm-m) and fresh shale (8 ohm-m) was sharp enough to trace the paleovalley across the entire parcel. Because the fill in that channel required compaction control before slab-on-grade construction, the team followed up with Proctor tests to nail down optimum moisture-density curves for the imported select fill. In the deeper sections, where the shale dipped below 9 meters, we recommended a footing investigation to verify allowable bearing pressures at the transition zone—better to confirm with a test pit and plate load than to guess from resistivity alone.

Local considerations

Arlington sits at roughly 180 meters elevation on the western edge of the Gulf Coastal Plain, and its growth—from 44,000 residents in 1970 to over 390,000 today—has pushed development into areas where subsurface drainage follows relict stream channels invisible at the surface. The combination of expansive Eagle Ford shale, unconsolidated terrace sands, and sporadic dissolution features in the underlying limestone creates a risk profile where undetected voids or soil pipes can trigger differential settlement long after construction wraps. A 2018 warehouse expansion near I-20 encountered a 2-meter cavity at 7 meters depth that conventional borings had missed entirely; the VES survey we ran afterward mapped the low-resistivity halo around the void—wet clay lining the dissolution pipe—and gave the structural engineer enough data to redesign the column spacing. Ignoring geophysical reconnaissance in these transitional geologic settings means accepting a level of stratigraphic uncertainty that even a dense CPT grid struggles to resolve alone.

Associated technical services

01

Vertical Electrical Sounding (VES)

Single-point Schlumberger array expanding to 300 meters. Best for mapping depth to bedrock, estimating water table elevation, or identifying clay-shale interfaces at a specific location.

02

2D Electrical Resistivity Tomography (ERT)

Multi-electrode lines with Wenner or dipole-dipole configuration, processed through solid inversion to produce continuous cross-sections. Ideal for utility corridors, pipeline routes, and pre-excavation void detection.

03

Combined Resistivity + MASW Surveys

Simultaneous acquisition where both resistivity and shear-wave velocity are needed for seismic site class determination under ASCE 7. We correlate the two datasets to reduce interpretation ambiguity in mixed soil-rock profiles.

Frequently asked questions

What depth can VES reach in the Arlington area?

With a maximum current-electrode spread of 300 meters, a Schlumberger VES typically reaches 45 to 60 meters depth in local soils. The practical limit depends on the resistivity contrast: in the low-resistivity Eagle Ford shale (under 10 ohm-m), signal penetration drops, and we may top out around 35 meters. For deeper targets, we switch to a dipole-dipole 2D line or complement the survey with seismic refraction.

How long does a resistivity survey take on a typical commercial lot?

A standard 2D line of 120 meters with 56 electrodes takes our two-person crew roughly 90 minutes to lay out and acquire, including electrode contact resistance checks. For a 2-hectare site with four parallel lines, expect one full field day plus one day for inversion processing and interpretation. Wet surface conditions after rain actually improve galvanic contact and can speed things up.

Can resistivity distinguish between saturated sand and clay in Arlington?

Yes, and that distinction is one of the main reasons clients bring us in. Saturated clean sand in the Woodbine or terrace deposits typically measures 30 to 80 ohm-m, while the weathered Eagle Ford clay-shale reads 5 to 15 ohm-m. The overlap zone around 15 ohm-m can be ambiguous—silty fine sand versus highly plastic clay—so in those cases we recommend a direct push CPT sounding at a selected anomaly location to calibrate the geoelectrical boundary with a physical log.

Do you need soil borings in addition to resistivity, or can resistivity replace some borings?

Resistivity doesn't replace borings—it makes them smarter. The geophysical data shows the continuity of layers between drill holes, so you can place borings on anomalies instead of on a rigid grid. On one Arlington ISD project, correlating the ERT lines with just five strategically placed borings gave us more stratigraphic control than the original design calling for eighteen evenly spaced holes. The key is always ground-truthing: at least two borings tied to the resistivity profile to anchor the resistivity-to-lithology conversion.
