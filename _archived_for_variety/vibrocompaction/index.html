Vibrocompaction Design in Arlington: Deep Compaction for Expansive North Texas Soils

Together, we solve the challenges of tomorrow.

Arlington sits at roughly 600 feet above sea level, straddling the boundary between the Blackland Prairie and the Eastern Cross Timbers. This geological transition means a single construction site can encounter hard shale bedrock, soft alluvial clays, and loose sandy lenses within the same footprint. When the 2015 Venetian Village earthquake swarm reminded North Texans that the region is not seismically inert, the conversation around soil densification changed. Loose granular deposits, particularly those saturated in the floodplains of the Trinity River tributaries, become a liability. Vibrocompaction design steps in precisely here. Instead of excavating and replacing poor material, the method rearranges granular particles into a denser state using depth vibrators, reducing the risk of sudden settlement. For Arlington projects over 3 stories, this approach often eliminates the need for deep foundations, provided the subsurface profile is suitable. We frequently pair vibro designs with a grain size analysis to confirm that the fines content stays below the 12 to 15 percent threshold where vibratory methods lose efficiency.

Achieving 70 percent relative density in Arlington's alluvial sands typically requires a triangular grid under 8-foot spacing, validated through post-compaction CPT verification.

Methodology and scope

The contrast between north Arlington, with its heavier Woodbine sandstone outcrops, and the southern sectors near the Trinity floodplain, where alluvium can extend beyond 30 feet, dictates entirely different compaction grids. In the south, the target is typically loose silty sands that demand a triangular probe pattern and tighter spacing to achieve the required relative density. Up north, encountering weathered shale fragments mixed with granular soil means the vibrator must penetrate a heterogeneous profile. The design phase requires a detailed assessment of grain size distribution, as excess fines can dampen the vibratory energy and prevent effective densification. In our experience, a preliminary CPT test provides the continuous tip resistance and sleeve friction data necessary to calibrate the compaction energy, allowing us to define the amperage draw and hold times before the first rig arrives on site. The goal is always to exceed 70 percent relative density, a benchmark that satisfies IBC Chapter 18 requirements for most commercial structures in the city.

Local considerations

A depth vibrator is not a simple probe. The rig carries a 130-kilowatt electric motor suspended from a crawler crane, with a follower tube that can extend past 60 feet. In Arlington, where summer temperatures routinely hit 100 degrees Fahrenheit, the hydraulic cooling systems work at their limit. The primary risk in vibrocompaction design is misidentifying the soil profile: if the design treats a silty clay layer as clean sand, the vibrator will churn without achieving compaction, wasting mobilization costs that can exceed $15,000 before the first column is completed. Another frequent issue is underestimating the lateral extent of treatment. Pore pressure generated during vibration can temporarily reduce effective stress, and without a proper drainage path designed into the grid, adjacent structures may experience settlement. The design must include a real-time data acquisition plan, tracking amperage versus depth to verify that the target energy is delivered uniformly. Skipping this step leaves the contractor blind to stratigraphic anomalies that could compromise the entire improvement zone beneath the footing.

Frequently asked questions

How much does vibrocompaction design cost for a standard Arlington commercial lot?

For a typical commercial parcel under 2 acres, the design package including grain size analysis, CPT data interpretation, grid layout, and verification planning runs between US$1,330 and US$5,350, depending on the depth of treatment and the number of pre-design borings required.

Does vibrocompaction work in the Eagle Ford Shale areas of Arlington?

Vibrocompaction is designed for granular soils with low fines content. The Eagle Ford Shale is a weathered, high-plasticity sedimentary rock that does not densify under vibration. In those zones, we recommend alternative methods such as over-excavation and replacement or drilled footings that reach competent material beneath the weathered zone.

How do you verify that the ground improvement actually worked?

We specify a post-treatment CPT program on a grid offset from the compaction points. Cone tip resistance must show a statistically significant increase over the pre-design values, with a minimum of 5 verification soundings per 10,000 square feet of treated area. The acceptance criteria are tied to the relative density target defined in the design phase.

What is the minimum depth you can treat with vibrocompaction in Arlington?

The practical minimum depth is about 10 feet. Shallower than that, the vibrator cannot generate sufficient confinement to achieve efficient particle rearrangement, and we typically opt for conventional roller compaction with a proof-roll verification instead.
